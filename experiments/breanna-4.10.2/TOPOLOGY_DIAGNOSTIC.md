# Synthetic contour topology diagnostics

The inspection accepts exactly three named Breanna pieces and one sewing-boundary contour per piece. It requires a first move, a final close, and explicit line or cubic operations in between. It reports the distance closed by the closing command, exact duplicate line endpoints, cubic segments whose four points coincide, and pairwise intersections in a flattened polyline. Nonconstant cubic loops are not called zero-length. Zero-length chords are counted but omitted from intersection-pair indexing so they do not create artificial nonadjacency.

Cubic subdivision uses de Casteljau at one half. For each resulting cubic, the maximum distance of its two interior control points to the finite endpoint chord bounds the cubic-to-chord distance in exact arithmetic: the curve lies in the convex hull, and distance to a convex set is convex. The reverse chord-to-curve distance is also bounded, since the continuous projection of the curve covers the chord between its endpoints. The report gives the largest calculated bound in mm and requires it to be at most the requested tolerance. Calculations use floating-point arithmetic without directed rounding, so this is not a formally certified bound. The segment count and subdivision depth are bounded.

Intersection and near-pair counts describe the approximation only. Near pairs are nonintersecting flattened nonadjacent edges within the sum of their individual bounds; adjacent pairs are not tested. Floating-point roundoff, adjacent overlaps, cusps, tangencies and near contacts are not fully resolved. Absence of reported crossings does not prove that a cubic boundary is simple, nor does a reported crossing alone prove an original cubic crossing. Unknown commands, invalid identities, extra contours, open paths and exhausted limits fail closed. No result establishes topology, cutting suitability, licensing clearance or production approval, and no output is connected to CAD, real data, plotting or cutting.
